a. Who is your target audience for your site? What will their goals be when visiting
your site?

The target audience for my website is mainly students at University of Wisconsin–Oshkosh
who need help with technology problems or just want easier access to tech resources. 
A lot of students struggle with things like laptops, software, printers, Wi-Fi, or 
setting up devices, so the goal of the site is to make getting help quick and simple.
When people visit the site, they will mostly want to: See what services are offered
book an appointment for help, find resources or troubleshooting tips, contact support
easily, and navigate the website quickly on both phones and computers
I wanted the website to feel simple, organized, and easy for students to use without feeling confusing or cluttered.



b. Carefully plan the design for your layout and navigation. Consider drawing sketch
wireframes. Consider how your design should look/function across screen sizes.

I planned the layout to be clean and easy to navigate so users can quickly find what they
need. The navigation bar is at the top of the page and includes links to the main pages 
like Home, Services, Booking, Resources, About, and Contact.On the homepage, I added a 
large welcome section along with service cards that explain the different types of tech 
support offered. I tried to separate content into sections so the page does not feel 
crowded. For smaller screens like phones and tablets, the navigation switches into a 
hamburger menu to make it easier to use on mobile devices. I also used responsive design
techniques like Flexbox and media queries so images and sections resize correctly on 
different screen sizes.My main goal with the layout was to keep everything simple, 
organized, and easy to use for college students.


c. Intentionally choose a color scheme for your site and document how you chose it.

I chose the color scheme from the official University of Wisconsin–Oshkosh branding page
because I wanted the website to match the school colors and feel connected to the campus.



d. Document at least three intentional design choices you make for your site with
your target audience/client in mind.

Mobile-Friendly Navigation
I added a hamburger menu for smaller screens because most students will probably use the site on their phones. This helps keep the navigation simple and easier to use on mobile devices.
Service Cards with Images and Icons
I used service cards with images and icons to help organize information and make the website more visually interesting. This also helps users quickly understand what services are available.
Consistent School Branding
I kept the UW-Oshkosh colors and theme consistent throughout the site to make it feel more professional and familiar to students.
I also added hover effects and spacing between sections to make the site feel smoother and less crowded.


e. Prior to submission, open your website in at least two different browsers. In your
memo, document which browsers you tested in, and provide a description of how
it looked in each? How similar or how different was the appearance in each?
Anything of notice to comment on?

I tested the website in both Google Chrome and Safari.
The website looked very similar in both browsers. The navigation, images, buttons, and 
layout all worked correctly, and the responsive design adjusted properly on different 
screen sizes. One thing I noticed was that Safari displayed some spacing and fonts a 
little differently compared to Chrome, but it was very minor and did not affect how the
site worked. Overall, the website stayed consistent and functional across both browsers.
